\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

HALO tests one proposition: acquisition configuration should calibrate evidence rather than be
normalized away. We build a sensor-granular encoder that defines frequency bands in physical hertz,
marks what a configuration cannot resolve, and learns without activity labels; we classify by a
transparent sequence of compatibility, admissibility, cosine ranking, voting, and sensor fusion; and
we enroll a new activity by appending rows to memory rather than updating parameters.

The proposition remains open. Our retrieval stage works --- it uses enrollment, and its ungated form
beats prototype and ridge heads on the same frozen features --- but the admissibility gate that
carries the thesis does not beat those controls, and its telemetry shows the veto never fires. We
report that rather than a headline, and we keep seven evaluation datasets sealed. The next
measurement is cheap and decisive: enroll on one device and query from another, which is the only
regime in which a two-sided admissibility test can express anything at all.
