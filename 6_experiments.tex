\subsection{Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

We evaluate whether HALO (i)~generalizes zero-shot to unseen datasets when scored against each dataset's \emph{own} label strings (cross-dataset zero-shot, ZS-XD), (ii)~transfers sample-efficiently under subject-disjoint few-shot supervision, and (iii)~runs on-device both offline and as a low-latency stream. \OUTDATED{The v1 open-set/closed-set zero-shot split and the ``severe out-of-domain'' category are removed; see the section banner for the full v1$\to$v2 protocol change.}

\textbf{Datasets.}
\label{sec:datasets}
We train jointly on 11 public HAR datasets (Table~\ref{tab:datasets}), including the free-living wrist corpus CAPTURE-24~\cite{chan2024capture24}, and evaluate on 6 held-out datasets \emph{never seen during any model's training}. HALO evaluates at each dataset's native sampling rate; classifier baselines use 20\,Hz resampled data via the LIMU-BERT pipeline (Section~\ref{sec:eval_protocol}).

\textbf{Baselines.}
\label{sec:baselines}
We compare HALO against self-supervised HAR encoders under the ZS-XD protocol (Table~\ref{tab:baselines}). \emph{Classifier-based models} (LiMU-BERT~\cite{limubert}, CrossHAR~\cite{crosshar}) produce a fixed-vocabulary classifier; to score them against a test dataset's own (possibly unseen) label strings we bridge their predictions to text via ConSE~\cite{conse}, projecting each classifier's softmax over training labels into the shared text-embedding space. We additionally plan to compare against \emph{UniMTS}~\cite{unimts} (a motion-time-series foundation model with released weights) and \emph{ssl-wearables}~\cite{sslwearables} (a large-scale self-supervised accelerometer model), both of which release checkpoints. \OUTDATED{The v1 comparison additionally included MOMENT, LanHAR, and LLaSA; these are dropped in v2 (MOMENT/LanHAR were dominated and LLaSA's published weights overlap our test sets). The UniMTS and ssl-wearables rows are pending; all baseline numbers are being re-measured.}

\textbf{Evaluation protocol.}
\label{sec:eval_protocol}
\emph{Cross-dataset zero-shot (ZS-XD).} Each held-out dataset is scored against its \emph{own} label strings---not a shared training-wide ontology---so a prediction counts only if it retrieves that dataset's exact activity name. HALO retrieves by cosine similarity to those label strings directly; classifier-based baselines are bridged to the same strings via ConSE~\cite{conse}, which projects each model's softmax over its training labels into the shared text-embedding space. Macro-F1 is the primary metric (accuracy reported alongside), since held-out label distributions are imbalanced.
\emph{Subject-disjoint few-shot.} For 1\% and 10\% supervision we fit on labeled data from subjects \emph{disjoint} from those evaluated, so no subject appears in both the fit and test splits; we report macro-F1.
\emph{Parity control.} To separate the architecture from its native-rate and rich-text advantages, we additionally evaluate HALO under baseline-equivalent inputs---common 20\,Hz resampling and neutral channel descriptions (Table~\ref{tab:fairness_control}).
\emph{Streaming.} On-device latency and a continuous real-world case study are reported in Sections~\ref{sec:scaling} and~\ref{sec:case_study}.
\OUTDATED{This replaces the v1 protocol, which scored a shared 87-label open-set/closed-set vocabulary with synonym-group credit for classifier baselines and per-patch majority voting for HALO, and subsampled few-shot data without enforcing subject disjointness---settings that leaked label and subject information across the split.}

\textbf{Implementation details.}
HALO is trained in a \emph{single} language-alignment stage over all 11 datasets---the encoder is trained from scratch, with no separate self-supervised pretraining stage. \OUTDATED{The v1 two-stage recipe (100 self-supervised pretraining epochs + 200 alignment epochs, MAE mask ratio 0.5, contrastive temperature 0.2) is superseded; the v2 recipe (single-stage, mixed full/causal temporal masks with an offline-to-online consistency term, and unified signal/text/physics augmentation) is pending the retrain and will be reported here.} Alignment hyperparameters retained from v1: learned temperature $\alpha_0{=}0.07$, soft-target temperature $\tau_s{=}0.5$, memory bank $Q{=}256$, gradient accumulation (micro-batch~32), and group-balanced sampling capped at $20\times$. HALO uses a fixed 1.0\,s evaluation patch by default (Section~\ref{sec:patch_sensitivity}). Training uses a single NVIDIA A100 80\,GB GPU; on-device profiling uses a smartphone (Section~\ref{sec:scaling}).

\subsubsection{Analysis of embedding space }
\label{sec:embedding_analysis}

Figure~\ref{fig:embedding_umap} visualizes the learned embedding space via UMAP~\cite{umap}. Semantically related groups (e.g., \textit{running}, \textit{stairs}, \textit{walking}) cluster together with tight cross-modal alignment: nearest-neighbor accuracy reaches 76.7\%, positive similarity 0.857, and the similarity gap between matched and unmatched pairs is 0.377. Stationary activities form a distinct cluster separated from locomotion, mirroring kinematic structure. Text centroids align closely with IMU counterparts, confirming the contrastive objective maps both modalities into a shared semantic space.

\subsection{Patch Size Sensitivity}
\label{sec:patch_sensitivity}


HALO uses a fixed 1.0\,s patch size for all results---no per-dataset tuning. Figure~\ref{fig:patch_sensitivity} shows that 1.0--1.25\,s patches consistently perform best; the fixed 1.0\,s choice is within 1.3~pp of the per-dataset optimum across all five datasets. This range aligns with typical gait cycle durations (0.8--1.2\,s), capturing approximately one full motion cycle per patch. Shorter patches (0.75\,s) degrade on complex-activity datasets like Opportunity, likely splitting compound activities across patch boundaries; longer patches (1.5--2.0\,s) show diminishing returns as they average over multiple motion phases. The sensitivity curve is relatively flat between 0.75--1.5\,s, and consistency across sampling rates (20--100\,Hz) confirms that seconds-based patching with adaptive pooling decouples temporal resolution from patch semantics.

\subsection{Scaling of Model Sizes}
\label{sec:scaling}

We train three model sizes---Tiny ($d{=}192$, 4 layers, 6M), Small ($d{=}384$, 8 layers, 35M), and Medium ($d{=}512$, 8 layers, 63M)---with identical training recipes, hyperparameters, and data to isolate the effect of model capacity.
Table~\ref{tab:scaling} shows Small is the sweet spot: Medium regresses by 8.2\,pp on ZS-O despite doubling parameters, achieving the highest \emph{validation} accuracy but the worst held-out generalization---classic overfitting to dataset-specific patterns rather than transferable motion representations. Tiny underfits, falling 13.2\,pp below Small on ZS-O, though it slightly outperforms Small at 10\% supervision (86.4\% vs.\ 85.3\%), suggesting smaller models benefit more from supervised regularization. The ${\sim}$13K-session corpus supports ${\sim}$35M parameters before overfitting dominates; larger HAR corpora could shift this ceiling upward.

\subsection{Real-World Case Study}
\label{sec:case_study}

\OUTDATED{Run with the v1 model; the specific numbers below (97.5\% accuracy, 30.35\,ms end-to-end latency, 21.8M deployed parameters) will be re-measured with the v2 checkpoint. The capability it demonstrates---offline personalization, on-device streaming inference, a mid-session sensor-placement change, and activity labels never seen during personalization---is exactly the reusable-foundation deployment of Section~\ref{sec:deployment} and is unchanged.}

We implement the full HALO pipeline as an iOS application and test under continuous placement and label shifts. As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:demo_setting}, the pipeline consists of four stages: (1)~SFT data collection on the smartphone, where the user performs each target activity for 30\,s per class; (2)~SFT head training on a laptop (under 2 minutes for 6 classes); (3)~weight transfer back to the device via a local network; and (4)~real-time inference. The demo covers three body locations (\textit{left pocket}, \textit{wrist}, \textit{upper arm}) with six seen classes (\textit{Sitting}, \textit{Standing}, \textit{Walking}, \textit{Running}, \textit{Jumping}, \textit{Lying}) and two unseen labels (\textit{Marching}, \textit{Jogging}) introduced at inference to test robustness beyond the SFT label space.

Across 40 inference windows (Figure~\ref{fig:demo_performance}), HALO achieves 97.5\% accuracy with only one transient error near the pocket-to-wrist transition, recovering immediately. The placement transition from pocket to wrist represents a significant sensor distribution shift mid-session, yet the model adapts within a single window. The unseen-label phase remains fully correct, confirming that the personalized head preserves semantic generalization even when inference-time labels differ from the SFT classes---the model correctly distinguishes ``Marching'' from ``Walking'' and ``Jogging'' from ``Running'' despite never training on these labels during SFT. Mean end-to-end pipeline latency is 30.35\,ms (P95: 33.66\,ms), higher than the isolated model benchmark (7.16\,ms, Table~\ref{tab:inference_results}) due to app-level overhead; memory stays within 73--78\,MB, and the deployed model is 21.8M parameters (${\sim}$34\,MB on disk)---practical for continuous on-device HAR.
