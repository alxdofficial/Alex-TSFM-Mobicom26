\section{Design of HALO}
\label{sec:design}

This section specifies the three components introduced in Section~\ref{sec:overview}: the
tokenizer that supplies HALO's calibrated inductive bias (Section~\ref{sec:tokenizer_design}),
the label-free objectives that train it (Section~\ref{sec:phaseA_design}), and the evidence
engine that turns the frozen representation into a recogniser over a run-time label set
(Section~\ref{sec:evidence_design}). Everything described here is implemented; where a design
choice has not yet been isolated by an experiment we say so and name the ablation that will
isolate it.

\subsubsection{Sensors, not channels}
\label{sec:sensor_grouping}

HALO's first commitment is that the unit of description is the \emph{sensor}, not the scalar
channel. Three co-located accelerometer axes on one device share a coordinate frame: they
rotate together, they experience the same gravity vector, and no physically realisable
transformation moves one without the others. Treating them as three independent series
discards this and, as Section~\ref{sec:no_grammar} argued, opens the door to per-channel text
acting as a corpus fingerprint. HALO therefore models a stream as a \emph{set of sensors},
each an accelerometer or gyroscope triad with its own device, placement, and gravity
convention. This grouping is what makes the augmentation physics correct as well: an $SO(3)$
rotation is applied jointly to an accelerometer triad and its co-located gyroscope, never to a
single axis, so every augmented sample corresponds to a device orientation that could actually
occur.

Accelerometer streams are gravity-aware. The quasi-static component below the analysis band is
split off as a \emph{signed} per-axis DC feature rather than removed, so posture survives.
Figure~\ref{fig:gravity} shows why this matters and, equally, where it stops helping. Averaged
over $200$ real waist-phone windows each, standing, sitting and lying deposit almost no energy
in the frequency path at all---peak band energy is around $3\times10^{-3}$, three orders of
magnitude below the gravity term---and what little structure remains separates no pair
distinctly, with all three pairwise cosines falling in a narrow $0.84$--$0.88$ range. In the
signed DC term the same windows separate sharply: standing and lying are essentially orthogonal
(cosine $-0.04$), because gravity has moved off one axis and onto two. A design that achieves orientation robustness by \emph{invariance}
discards precisely this quantity, and with it the distinction between lying down and standing
still---an activity pair that matters in every sleep, posture and fall application. The same
figure is honest about the limit: standing and sitting remain close in DC ($0.99$) at a waist
placement, so that pair is not recoverable this way and must come from elsewhere.
Because gravity conventions differ across platforms, the convention is declared in the sensor's
text (below) and a gravity-removal augmentation is applied with low probability so the encoder
sees both conventions for the same underlying motion.

\subsubsection{A physical-frequency constant-$Q$ filterbank}
\label{sec:filterbank}

Each patch of each channel is turned into one token entirely in the physical-frequency domain
(Figure~\ref{fig:tokenizer}). The patch is Hann-windowed and its DC component removed, then
transformed by a zero-padded real DFT of fixed length $S{=}256$. The zero-padding is what makes
this rate-agnostic: for a patch sampled at rate $r$, DFT bin $m$ corresponds to the physical
frequency $\phi[m] = mr/S$, so the \emph{same} matrix of bin indices maps to different Hz grids
at different rates, and the readout can be defined once in Hz. That readout is a constant-$Q$
Gaussian filterbank~\cite{constantq,constantqfast} with $K{=}32$ centers log-spaced over
$[0.3, 15]$\,Hz and $\sigma_k = c_k/2Q$ at $Q{=}4$. The band limits are physically motivated
rather than tuned: gait cadence occupies $0.5$--$3$\,Hz, limb and hand motion contribute
harmonics to roughly $10$--$12$\,Hz, and content below $0.3$\,Hz is gravity, which the signed
DC feature already carries. Band energies are window-normalised so they do not depend on patch
length, log-compressed, and per-band standardised with statistics calibrated once before
training.

Figure~\ref{fig:rateinv} shows this working on real data rather than asserting it. One
$1.5$\,s wrist patch of walking is resampled to $100$, $50$, and $20$\,Hz and passed through the
tokenizer at its own rate. As sample arrays the three are entirely different objects---$150$,
$75$, and $30$ numbers---but the band energies they produce agree to cosine $0.997$ and $0.996$
over the bands all three can observe, and the six bands the $20$\,Hz stream cannot legally
measure are masked rather than filled in.

The consequence is that rate-invariance is a property of the construction, not of a learned
approximation: a $2$\,Hz gait rhythm deposits its energy in the same band whether the device
sampled at $20$ or $100$\,Hz, and because no interpolation exists in the path there is no
aliasing to introduce and no fabricated sample to explain away. Note the distinction from
rate-\emph{equivariance}~\cite{flowstate}: the model is never told the rate in order to compare
two windows, because band $k$ denotes the same physical frequency in both. That is what allows
exemplars from different devices to occupy one memory (Section~\ref{sec:evidence_design})
without a per-configuration index. Zero-padding beyond the true
patch length is frequency-domain interpolation only and does not alter band energies; we verified
that $S{=}256$ and $S{=}512$ give band-energy cosine $1.000000$ and identical masks at $20$,
$25$, $50$, and $100$\,Hz. Patch lengths exceeding $S$ raise an error rather than silently
truncating.

\subsubsection{Observability: say what could not be measured}
\label{sec:masks}

Rate-invariance would be dishonest without a second mechanism. A $20$\,Hz stream simply cannot
report energy at $12$\,Hz, and a $0.4$\,s patch cannot resolve a $0.3$\,Hz band. Rather than
emitting a small number that the encoder might read as ``no energy here,'' each token carries
two masks. The \emph{Nyquist observability} mask marks band $k$ observable only if
$c_k + 2\sigma_k \le 0.9\,(r/2)$, a $10\%$ guard below Nyquist so a band's Gaussian tail does
not straddle the aliasing edge; at $20$\,Hz this leaves $26$ of $32$ bands, at $25$\,Hz $27$,
and at $50$ and $100$\,Hz all $32$. The \emph{resolution} mask marks band $k$ resolved only
if at least one full cycle fits in the patch, $c_k \ge 1/D$ for patch duration $D$; a $0.5$\,s
patch resolves $16$ of $32$ bands and a $1.5$\,s patch $25$. A band that is present but blurry
is flagged, not zeroed. Streams whose distributed form was resampled by their publisher take
the Nyquist bound from the \emph{acquisition} rate rather than the stored rate---upsampling
cannot create information---which in our corpus applies to the $25$\,Hz earbud IMU stored on a
$50$\,Hz grid.

\subsubsection{Factored language conditioning}
\label{sec:conditioning}

Tokens produced by a weight-shared tokenizer are agnostic to what produced them, so acquisition
context must be injected. HALO factors that context in two, deliberately:

\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item a \textbf{role} string per channel that names \emph{only} the axis---\texttt{"x"},
\texttt{"y"}, \texttt{"z"}---and is therefore constant across the entire corpus;
\item a \textbf{sensor identity} string per sensor carrying device, modality, placement, and
gravity convention, e.g.\ \texttt{"a smartwatch}\allowbreak\ \texttt{accelerometer on the}
\allowbreak\ \texttt{wrist; includes gravity"}, or \texttt{"a phone gyroscope on a}
\allowbreak\ \texttt{trouser pocket"}.
\end{itemize}

Both are embedded by the same frozen sentence encoder~\cite{sbert} and fused into the token,
the sensor embedding through a gated residual initialised near zero so conditioning starts as a
gentle bias and grows only if it earns its place. The factorisation is the point. Placement,
device, and modality appear \emph{only} in the sensor string and axis \emph{only} in the role
string, so no acquisition fact is injected twice when the two are summed. More importantly,
the description a channel receives is now shared by every channel of that sensor and by every
sensor of that kind across the corpus, which is precisely what makes it too coarse to identify
a dataset. Numeric metadata---sampling rate, patch duration---is deliberately \emph{absent}
from the text, because a frozen sentence encoder represents numbers unreliably; those values
enter numerically through the bin$\to$Hz map and the observability masks instead. During
training the sensor strings are paraphrased and, with some probability, dropped to a generic
string, so the encoder must tolerate an unfamiliar or missing description at deployment.

Whether the factorisation actually buys the anti-memorisation property it is designed for is an
empirical question, not a design claim, and Section~\ref{sec:experiments} tests it directly
against a per-channel-text variant and a layout-locked variant
(Tables~\ref{tab:tokenizer_ablation} and~\ref{tab:identity_probe}).

\subsubsection{Set encoder over physical time}
\label{sec:encoder}

Conditioned tokens enter a dual-branch transformer~\cite{transformer} that alternates \emph{temporal}
self-attention within a channel and \emph{cross-channel} self-attention within a patch, with
channel masks so absent or padded channels never contribute. Channels carry no positional
index at all---their identity is text---so the encoder is permutation- and count-invariant over
channels by construction, and a device with three channels and one with twelve are the same
kind of input. Temporal positions use rotary embeddings~\cite{rope} keyed to \emph{physical
time} in seconds rather than patch index, with periods spanning $0.4$--$600$\,s. This is the
counterpart of the filterbank on the time axis: a $1.2$\,s gesture receives the same relative
temporal code regardless of sampling rate or patch length, and because rotary positions are
relative they remain exact under a key/value cache. The default configuration is $d{=}256$ with
$6$ layers and $8$ heads ($7.17$M parameters), reading $6$\,s windows tokenised at two
resolutions drawn per batch from $0.4$--$0.8$\,s and $1.0$--$1.5$\,s.

\textbf{Streaming.} Because the tokenizer uses no cross-patch statistics and the positions are
relative, the only component mixing information across time is temporal self-attention, so
offline and causal operation differ by nothing but the attention mask; the encoder implements
both. We report this as a design affordance rather than a claim: this submission does not train
a causal arm, distil offline to online, or measure streaming equivalence, and
Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} lists it as future work.

\subsection{Phase A: Label-Free Representation Pretraining}
\label{sec:phaseA_design}

Phase A trains the tokenizer and encoder with objectives that never observe an activity label.
This is a change of principle, not an economy: as long as the objective is a closed-vocabulary
label prediction, the model is rewarded for exploiting whatever predicts labels within the
training corpora, and Section~\ref{sec:no_grammar} explained why that is the failure we most
need to avoid. Removing labels from the loss also unlocks unlabelled and weakly labelled data,
which is where the scale in wearables actually is.

The objective is a weighted sum of five terms plus one rail.

\textbf{(A1) Masked prediction, with two targets.} A validity-aware mask over physical time
intervals hides a subset of tokens; the encoder predicts, at masked positions, both the
\emph{fixed} filterbank features of the hidden content and the \emph{contextual latents} that a
stop-gradient EMA teacher~\cite{byol,data2vec,ijepa} produces from the unmasked window. The
fixed target grounds the representation in physical quantities; the latent target lets the
representation become more abstract than its own input features. The same cross-resolution mask
is used for both so the two views cannot leak into each other, and the teacher is updated only
after an optimizer step (decay $0.996$) and is checkpointed with the model.

\textbf{(A2) VICReg over augmented views.} Two independently augmented views of the same window
are pulled together under VICReg~\cite{vicreg}---invariance, variance, and covariance terms
reported separately---rather than under a contrastive loss. The reason is specific to this
corpus: with NT-Xent, other windows in the batch are declared negatives, and in a multi-dataset
corpus the batch is full of windows of the same activity from other subjects and other
datasets, so a contrastive objective actively pushes apart things that should be together.
VICReg needs no negatives. SimCLR~\cite{simclr} and supervised
contrastive~\cite{supcon} remain selectable as controls. Augmentations span jitter, scaling,
time shift and warp, magnitude warp, window cropping and length jitter, rate change, channel
dropout, joint $SO(3)$ rotation of co-located triads, gravity add/remove, and the text
augmentations of Section~\ref{sec:conditioning}. Text augmentation is applied with a
\emph{shared} seed across the two views, so that a view pair never differs only in whether its
configuration was described---an asymmetry that would otherwise teach the encoder to ignore
conditioning entirely.

\textbf{(A3) Time--frequency consistency.} An auxiliary time-domain encoder reads the raw
samples of the same window, and its pooled output is pulled toward the main (frequency-domain)
encoder's under a separate VICReg head. This is inspired by TF-C~\cite{tfc} but is not faithful
to it---there is no time-to-time contrastive term, and the time encoder is discarded at
inference---so we describe it as a cross-domain consistency rail rather than as TF-C.

\textbf{(A4) Cross-placement consistency.} Where the corpus contains \emph{verified
simultaneous} recordings---the same physical event captured at two or more body placements at
the same instant---those pairs form an additional positive source at low weight and under a
quota, so that the six-placement recordings cannot dominate. Positive identity comes from
explicit physical-event identifiers written during conversion and carried into the pretraining
index; it is never inferred from equal array lengths or row alignment, and never from an
activity label. The training split contains $17{,}387$ paired events over $55{,}270$ paired
windows.

\textbf{(A5) A small physical grounding rail.} Two self-derived, augmentation-aware targets
(dominant cadence and a covariance eigenstructure ratio) are regressed at low weight. They use
no labels and act as a sanity anchor rather than a driver.

Sampling is a per-window temperature draw with $P(\text{dataset}) \propto n^{\alpha}$,
$\alpha{=}0.5$, which is the label-free counterpart of class balancing: a $144$k-window
free-living corpus must not swamp a $1$k-window laboratory one, but neither should every source
be forced to equal mass. Model selection uses subject-disjoint validation $k$-NN balanced
accuracy---labels are used here, as a \emph{metric}, never in the loss. Per-objective gradient
norms, VICReg component values, minimum feature standard deviation, effective rank, and
positive similarity by pair type are logged every step, and non-finite values or representation
collapse fail the run rather than being smoothed over.

\subsection{Phase B: The Evidence Engine}
\label{sec:evidence_design}

The encoder is frozen. What remains is to turn its representation into a decision over label
strings the deployment supplies at run time---without the single sensor-to-text projection that
Section~\ref{sec:geometry} showed to be mis-specified.

\textbf{Memory.} Every labelled window available---from the training corpus and, optionally,
from the target deployment---is encoded once and written to a versioned table. An entry stores
the patch vector together with its label, subject, dataset and acquisition configuration,
source window, physical event, sensor group, patch center time and duration, resolution, and
validity. Sensor streams are encoded separately and a session's patches form a query set. The
table is append-only: adding a class, a placement, or a device is a write, not a training run.
Every Phase-B artifact is bound to the exact Phase-A checkpoint, corpus, bank fingerprint,
patch schema, and candidate-text vocabulary it was built from, and a behavioural probe rejects a
bank whose encoder has since changed---silently stale memory is the most dangerous failure mode
this design has.

\textbf{Retrieval.} Each query patch retrieves independently, by similarity in \emph{sensor}
space, under learned projections rather than hard dimension slices; each projection defines its
own subspace with its own retrieval and its own evidence contribution, and head collapse is
resisted by output decorrelation, projection diversity, and contribution-usage regularisation.
An EMA copy of the projectors indexes memory while the online projectors score what comes back.
During training the query's own window, its physical event, and its subject are excluded from
retrieval, and contributions are capped per source window and per label so a single long
recording cannot dominate a vote. Evidence is aggregated with physical-duration normalisation
within a resolution before resolutions are combined, so the denser short-patch grid does not
receive an automatic vote-count advantage.

\textbf{Candidate-aware decoding.} Retrieved evidence is not simply averaged. A small set
transformer contextualises query and evidence tokens together under learned structural roles
(\textsc{query}, \textsc{evidence}, \textsc{candidate}), then refines each evidence item's
label text as a residual in frozen sentence space---this is what lets a neighbour labelled
\emph{walking} but retrieved in a stair-climbing context contribute as
\emph{walking upstairs}---and pools evidence as a residual on the retrieval prior. Candidate
tokens carry frozen label text plus a candidate role and attend permutation-equivariantly over
the fixed evidence states, so neither candidate order nor evidence order carries meaning.
Positions are window-relative continuous-time Fourier features, never a global sequence index,
because relative time \emph{across} windows is meaningless. Every sublayer is gated by a
LayerScale initialised near zero and the refinement and pooling residuals are zero-initialised,
so the decoder at initialisation is exactly the untrained retrieval-plus-text-ensemble
mechanism; any change it makes is attributable to training.

\textbf{Confidence and rejection.} The decoder emits non-negative evidence per candidate with
attribution to the patches, sensors, subspaces, and retrieved examples that produced it, which
is what allows a prediction to be inspected rather than merely reported. There is no
\textsc{unknown} candidate; instead a rejection confidence is estimated
separately~\cite{selectiveclass,edl} from absolute evidence, retrieval density, patch and sensor
agreement, normalised vote entropy, and the leading-candidate margin, and calibrated on
truth-absent validation episodes with risk/coverage reported. Truth-present episodes require
support, so always-reject is not a solution.

\textbf{Episodic training.} The decoder is trained on episodes that vary, independently: the
candidate budget ($10$--$100\%$ of the vocabulary); the number of true-label memory exemplars
($0$, $1$, $2$, $4$, $8$, or all); the support available for competing labels; whether memory
is same-configuration, cross-configuration only, or missing the query's configuration entirely;
the kind of distractors present (random, language-near, motion-family-near, physically
confusable); and whether the truth is present at all. Complete label \emph{families} are held
out of decoder training and reserved for model selection, so candidate-text reasoning is
always tested on concepts the decoder never fit.

Because the decoder is identity-at-initialisation, every trained arm has an exact untrained
control, and Section~\ref{sec:experiments} reports the two side by side rather than reporting
the trained arm alone.

\subsection{Deployment}
\label{sec:deployment}

A deployment is two pieces of configuration and no code. The \emph{sensor layout} is one
description string per sensor; the \emph{candidate set} is a list of activity names. Both are
embedded once by the frozen sentence encoder and cached, so run-time cost is one encoder pass
plus a retrieval, with no per-inference text cost. Changing the label set, or moving to a
different device, recomputes a cache.

The label-efficiency dial is the memory. At $k{=}0$ the target deployment contributes nothing
and evidence comes from corpus exemplars and the candidate strings. At $k{>}0$ the user
demonstrates each activity a few times; each demonstration costs one forward pass, is appended
to the bank, and is immediately available---no gradient step, no retraining cycle, no
revalidation of weights that did not move, and no requirement that the data leave the device.
Where labels are plentiful, the same frozen features support a conventional supervised head. We
expose this behind a small \texttt{predict()} interface and package the encoder for on-device
execution (Core~ML), demonstrated in the iOS case study of Section~\ref{sec:case_study}.
