\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Cross-dataset and foundation models for wearable sensing.}
DeepConvLSTM~\cite{deepconvlstm} is representative of supervised HAR models trained for one
benchmark. Later work addresses selected sources of variation~\cite{cosmo,unihar,clusterfl} or
pretrains transferable encoders. LiMU-BERT~\cite{limubert} uses masked reconstruction at
$20$\,Hz, CrossHAR~\cite{crosshar} adds a contrastive objective, and
ssl-wearables~\cite{sslwearables} scales self-supervision on wrist accelerometry. Scaling Wearable
Models~\cite{lsm1} studies population-scale pretraining and imputation; LSM-2~\cite{lsm2}
specifically targets incomplete wearable observations. RelCon~\cite{relcon} learns relative motion
similarity from roughly one billion sensor segments, and SensorLM~\cite{sensorlm} pairs wearable
signals with language. Tartan IMU~\cite{tartanimu} studies inertial positioning, while general
time-series architectures include MOMENT and PatchTST~\cite{moment,patchtst}. Mitra is not included
in this group because it is a tabular, rather than time-series, foundation model~\cite{mitra}.

The controlled Inertia-1 study varies modality, placement, rate, and window length~\cite{inertia1};
the recent HAR foundation-model survey provides a broader taxonomy~\cite{harfmsurvey}. We cite the
study for its controlled findings and the survey only for the landscape. HALO's narrower question is
whether acquisition configuration can calibrate which retrieved evidence is allowed to vote.

\textbf{Sampling and acquisition context.}
Rate-invariant autoencoding disentangles order-preserving temporal warp into rate parameters and a
rate-invariant latent~\cite{rateinvae}. FlowState combines a state-space model with a functional
basis decoder and dynamic timescale adjustment for sampling-rate-equivariant
forecasting~\cite{flowstate}. Universal Spectral Tokenization accepts native wavelength grids in
astronomy and proposes extension to other sampled domains; it is adjacent inspiration, not evidence
on wearable HAR~\cite{spectraltok}. MoPFormer learns IMU motion primitives after resampling its
datasets to $100$\,Hz~\cite{mopformer}. These systems therefore should not be summarized as sharing
one rate-normalization strategy.

Other work supplies acquisition context explicitly. GOAT~\cite{goat} uses activity and body-position
text, ZeroHAR~\cite{zerohar} uses categorical sensor context, and CHARM was introduced as a preprint
and subsequently presented in the peer-reviewed SensorsVoice work~\cite{charm,sensorsvoice}.
SensorLLM~\cite{sensorllm} and oneHAR~\cite{onehar} also use language for sensor context, whereas
UniMTS~\cite{unimts} represents placement as a graph and uses rotation augmentation. HALO does not
claim that sensor descriptions are new. Its contribution is to separate a declared description
from measured acquisition statistics and then use configuration to calibrate retrieval
admissibility.

\textbf{Retrieval and label-efficient recognition.}
Template matching has long been used for gestures~\cite{uwave,junker,warpinglcss}. Matching Networks
and Prototypical Networks learn or use comparison rules under episodic tasks~\cite{matchingnet,protonet}.
Language and vision systems augment parametric models with external non-parametric
memory~\cite{knnlm,rag,retro,knnvision,reco}; not all of these systems keep the parametric model
frozen. Memory-modular classification studies adding classes by changing memory
contents~\cite{memorymodular}.

In sensing, RAG-HAR retrieves signal descriptors for an LLM~\cite{raghar}, and ZARA is a
training-free, evidence-grounded LLM agent~\cite{zara}. TimEE and TIC-FM perform zero- or
few-example time-series classification in context~\cite{timee,ticfm}. These are not evidence for a
target-example $k$ curve without parameter updates unless such a curve is explicitly reported.
Appendix~\ref{app:entailment} therefore separates open-label evaluation, use of target examples,
and no-update enrollment instead of collapsing them into one checkmark.
