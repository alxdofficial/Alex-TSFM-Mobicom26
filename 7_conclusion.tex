
\section{Conclusion and Discussion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We argued that the obstacle to a reusable IMU foundation model is not a shortage of data but
two structural facts about the modality. Inertial signals admit no closed grammar: the map from
a motion to a recording is composed at deployment time from continuous, composable nuisance
factors, so cross-configuration compatibility has to be supplied as calibrated inductive bias
rather than accumulated as coverage. And sensor similarity does not track linguistic
similarity---measured on our own vocabulary, the ordering is not merely noisy but inverted---so
projecting sensor features into a sentence-embedding space is a mis-specified target that
rewards dataset memorisation. HALO responds with a tokenizer that commits to the physics that
does not vary (rigid sensor triads, a physical-frequency axis, signed gravity) and defers
everything that does to per-sensor language and per-token observability masks; and with an
evidence engine that retrieves in sensor space and votes in label space, so the two geometries
never meet in one projection. Representation pretraining uses no activity labels at all;
labels enter only as annotations on retrieved memory entries, which is what makes the amount of
target supervision a dial rather than a mode.

\textbf{What we are not claiming.} Three things, stated plainly so they are not read into the
work. We do not claim that accepting a variable number or order of channels is a contribution;
padding with a mask is one line of preprocessing, and our own corpus spans only three to six
channels per device. We do not claim that recognising unseen label \emph{strings} is a
contribution; ConSE has done that since 2014, which is exactly why we equip every
closed-vocabulary baseline with it. And where labels are plentiful and a single fixed
configuration is known in advance, we do not expect retrieval to beat supervised fitting---that
is a solved problem and the honest place to concede it is here rather than in a rebuttal.

\textbf{Limitations.} Our training corpus is roughly $570$ hours of windowed signal, several orders of magnitude
smaller than the largest released accelerometer models, and no design choice compensates for
that on tasks where scale is what matters. The evidence engine's ceiling is its encoder: if
retrieval returns neighbours of the wrong activity, no decoder recovers, and we measure
retrieval purity precisely because it bounds everything downstream. The cross-placement
objective depends on datasets with verified simultaneous recordings, of which there are few.
Windows are single-activity, so the model learns to recognise segments and not to find
boundaries. And the encoder supports causal operation but this submission does not train or
measure it, so streaming remains a design affordance rather than a demonstrated capability.

\textbf{Future work.} Four directions follow directly. \emph{Dense supervision}: per-frame
labels from concatenated or free-living streams would teach onset and transition detection,
which single-activity windows cannot. \emph{Streaming, properly}: train a causal arm, distil
offline to online, and verify streaming equivalence, converting the affordance into a claim.
\emph{Scale on the axis that is actually free}: because Phase~A needs no labels, the corpus can
grow into unlabelled free-living data, which is where the hours are. \emph{Controlled-shift
evaluation}: the corpus contains simultaneous multi-placement recordings of the same person at
the same instant, which isolate placement from subject, session, and activity---a cleanly
controlled axis that a blended cross-dataset average hides, and the right way to measure the
compatibility claim one nuisance factor at a time.

Finally, a note on what a negative result would mean. The label-efficiency curve
(Section~\ref{sec:label_efficiency}) has a pre-registered kill criterion: if a linear probe on
frozen features dominates retrieval at every budget including a single example per class, then
the non-parametric mechanism has no regime of its own and the argument reduces to its
operational properties---one encoder across many deployments, append-only updates without
revalidation, on-device enrollment, and inspectable decisions. Those are real, but they are
engineering properties, and we would say so rather than reframe the metric.
