
\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

HALO sits at the intersection of three literatures, and in each some part of what we do already
exists. We name the closest work and state what is left.

\textbf{Cross-dataset and foundation models for IMU sensing.}
Deep HAR has moved from single-dataset CNN--LSTM models~\cite{deepconvlstm} through methods
attacking one axis of heterogeneity at a time~\cite{cosmo,unihar,clusterfl} to self-supervised
encoders intended to transfer: LiMU-BERT~\cite{limubert} pretrains by span-masked reconstruction
at $20$\,Hz, CrossHAR~\cite{crosshar} adds contrastive learning, and
ssl-wearables~\cite{sslwearables} scales multi-task self-supervision to ${\sim}700$k person-days
of wrist accelerometry. A second wave targets foundation scale, establishing the label-efficiency
argument and learning from incomplete recordings~\cite{lsm1,lsm2}, learning motion-similarity
metrics at ${\sim}10^9$ windows~\cite{relcon}, and pairing sensor data with
captions~\cite{sensorlm}; general time-series and tabular models~\cite{moment,patchtst,mitra}
trade domain bias for breadth, and Inertia-1~\cite{inertia1} runs a controlled lifecycle study
over modality, placement, rate, and window length~\cite{harfmsurvey}. \emph{What is left:} all of
these treat sampling rate as a preprocessing decision to be normalised away. We argue that is
where the information loss happens, and Section~\ref{sec:bias_experiments} measures it.

\textbf{Absorbing acquisition heterogeneity: resolution and language.}
Two lines address the input side. The first, and the closest to our L1 mechanism, is not in HAR:
rate-invariant autoencoding~\cite{rateinvae} quotients out temporal warping,
FlowState~\cite{flowstate} builds a sampling-rate-\emph{equivariant} forecaster, and universal
spectral tokenization~\cite{spectraltok} ingests native, irregular grids without resampling.
Within IMU sensing, MoPFormer~\cite{mopformer} tokenizes into learned motion primitives but
resamples every dataset to $100$\,Hz first, so its codebook is defined over a fixed-rate grid.
The second describes the sensor in language, and is crowded: GOAT~\cite{goat} supervises over
labels and on-body positions; ZeroHAR~\cite{zerohar} conditions on categorical sensor context;
CHARM and its successor~\cite{charm,sensorsvoice} feed channel-level descriptions into a
channel-order-equivariant transformer, finding text conditioning on channel identity among their
largest drivers; SensorLLM~\cite{sensorllm} and oneHAR~\cite{onehar} take related routes;
UniMTS~\cite{unimts} instead encodes placement structurally as a joint graph with
rotation-invariant augmentation. \emph{What is left, honestly:} ``sensor identity as natural
language'' is not ours to claim, and our differences are narrower than the general idea. FlowState
conditions on the rate---it is equivariant, adapting its computation---whereas HALO's features
are \emph{anchored} to physical Hz, so no conditioning is required to compare across devices. And
the channel-description line~\cite{charm,sensorsvoice,sensorllm} attaches free text \emph{per
channel}, exactly the configuration whose corpus-identifiability we argue against and test
(Table~\ref{tab:tokenizer_ablation}); ZeroHAR's attributes are a fixed closed set, whereas free
text composes to descriptions never seen.

\textbf{Retrieval, memory, and few-shot recognition.}
Deciding by comparison to stored examples is old in this field and new in its current form. The
ubicomp lineage recognises gestures by template~\cite{uwave,junker,warpinglcss}; the modern one
is episodic and parametric~\cite{matchingnet,protonet}, and in language and vision attaches
non-parametric memory to a frozen model~\cite{knnlm,rag,retro,knnvision,reco}. Memory-modular
classification~\cite{memorymodular} states our Phase-B property as its contribution: generalise
to new classes by replacing memory contents, without retraining. In sensing,
RAG-HAR~\cite{raghar} retrieves over signal descriptors and lets an LLM decide;
ZARA~\cite{zara} retrieves multi-sensor evidence into an LLM agent, under the same
``evidence-grounded'' name we use for a different mechanism; and in-context time-series
classifiers~\cite{timee,ticfm} collapse zero- and few-shot into one forward pass.
\emph{What is left:} not ``we retrieve,'' but that retrieval happens where similarity is
\emph{sensor} similarity between encoded exemplar \emph{patches}, from an encoder conditioned on
acquisition configuration---which is what lets a wrist query at an unusual rate retrieve
comparable evidence, and which descriptor-space, LLM-agent, and CLIP-space retrieval cannot do.
To that we add calibrated rejection~\cite{selectiveclass,edl}.
Section~\ref{sec:label_efficiency} reports the control that decides whether the distinction is
real: the same memory mechanism on a competitor's encoder.

\subsection{The Combination, and Why It Is Not a List}
\label{sec:combination}

Table~\ref{tab:coverage} summarises which axes the closest systems address. No prior system
addresses all of them, which is true and also the least interesting thing to say: ``we combine
more components'' is not a contribution. Variable channel counts, variable rates, and language as
a bridge to an open vocabulary all have known solutions, and we claim none as novel. Our claim is
different---that physical-Hz \emph{anchoring} is what makes a single, flat, cross-device memory
well-posed in the first place, an \emph{enabling} result rather than an assembly. Such a memory
requires that a query encoded on one device and an exemplar encoded on another be comparable
\emph{as vectors}, with no per-configuration index to reconcile them; neither resampling nor
rate-equivariance provides that, which is why existing retrieval-based sensing systems retrieve
somewhere else---descriptor space~\cite{raghar}, an LLM agent~\cite{zara}, CLIP
space~\cite{sensorlm}---and why none needs the property we claim. Once it is in view the
remaining choices stop looking like options: they are \emph{mutually entailed}, so the set
follows from L1 and L2 rather than being a collection of independently attractive features
(Appendix~\ref{app:entailment}). Section~\ref{sec:bias_experiments} tests this directly, and
because ``it is just a combination'' is an empirical objection, the conventional assembly is run
as a rival arm.

\textbf{A note on scale.} We do not claim the scale-first programme fails on its own terms: on
population-scale health outcomes, models trained on tens of millions of
hours~\cite{sslwearables,lsm1,sensorlm} are the right instrument and we would not beat them. The
disagreement is about which axis a corpus can close. Scale reduces surprise along the axes a
corpus \emph{samples}---subjects, activities, sessions---but cannot close the axes
\emph{generated at deployment time} by a composable chain of mounting, placement, filtering, and
convention choices, because those are not drawn from the corpus distribution at all. Our position
is not ``less data, cleverer model''; it is that the two axes call for different remedies, and
the second is under-served because it is invisible to the benchmark protocols the field uses.
